
Radiation carries momentum.

Thus, if a medium absorbs radiation, the radiation exerts a force on the medium.


---
## Radiation flux vector


$$
\vec{F}_\nu = \int I_\nu \, \hat{n} \, d\Omega,
$$
where
- $\hat{n}$: unit vector along the direction of the ray


---
## Radiation momentum vector

Since the momentum of a photon is $E/c$, 

the vector momentum per unit area per unit time per unit path length absorbed by the medium is:

$$ 
\vec{\mathcal{F}} = \frac{1}{c}\int \alpha_\nu \, \vec{F}_\nu \, d\nu.
$$


---
## Radiation force

Since $dV = dA \, ds$,

the force per unit volume imparted onto the medium by the radiation field $\vec{f}$ is:

$$
\vec{f} = \frac{1}{c} \int \kappa_\nu \, \vec{F}_\nu \, d\nu.
$$

---

>[!caution] Underlying assumptions!
>The absorption coefficient is isotropic. 
>Thus, for the isotropic radiation, no momentum is imparted by the radiation.
