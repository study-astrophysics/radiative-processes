
A radiation which is itself in thermal equilibrium.

## Thought experiment

A vacuum enclosure at temperature $T$, and do not let radiation in or out until equilibrium has been achieved.

Now, open a very small hole so that we can keep the equilibrium, and measure the radiation.

We can derive some properties.

>[!note] The number of photons will adjust itself in equilibrium at temperature $T$.

Since photons are massless, they can be created and destroyed in arbitrary numbers by the walls of the container. (For now, let's ignore *self-interaction* between photons.)

There is no conservative law of photon number, the number of photons will adjust itself in equilibrium at $T$. 


>[!note] $I_\nu$ is independent of the properties of the enclosure and depends only on the temperature.


Consider joining the container to another one of arbitrary shape and placing a filter, which passes a single frequency $\nu$ but no others.

If $I_\nu \neq I_\nu'$, then energy will flow spontaneously between the two. 

Since these are at the same temperature, this violates the second law of thermodynamics.

 $$
 \therefore I_\nu = (\mathrm{universal \ function \ of\ }T \mathrm{\ and \ }\nu  )\equiv B_\nu(T)
 $$
where
- $B_\nu (T)$: Planck function

Also, it is also isotropic:


$$
I_\nu \neq I_\nu (\Omega).
$$
