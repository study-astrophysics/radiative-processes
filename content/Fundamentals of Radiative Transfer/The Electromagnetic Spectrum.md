

Electromagnetic radiation can be decomposed into a spectrum of constituent components.


The spectrum corresponds to waves of various wavelengths and frequencies, related by $\lambda \nu = c$. 
-  $\nu$: frequency of the wave
- $\lambda$: its wavelength
- $c=2.998 \times 10^{10} \mathrm{ \ cm \ s^{-1}}$: free space velocity of light.


For convenience, we have given the energy $E = h\nu$ and temperature $T= E/k$ associated with each wavelength. 
- $h = 6.625 \times 10^{-27} \mathrm{ \ erg \ s}$: Planck's constant
- $k=1.38\times 10^{-16} \mathrm{\ erg \ K^{-1}}$: Boltmann's constant

Each field has its own unit to indicate the wavelength or frequency of the electromagnetic spectrum. For instance,
- In the X-ray astronomy, keV (kilo electron-volt) is usually used to say the X-ray photon's energy (as well as its frequency scale).
- In the optical, people use Angstrom ($1 \mathring{\mathrm{A}} = 10^{-10} \mathrm{\ m}$ ).
